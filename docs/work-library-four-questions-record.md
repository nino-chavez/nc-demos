# The Chiropractor's Four Questions

## Decision

Keep the platform recommendation only after its load-bearing claims have been re-derived from readable sources and their confidence is visible to the client.

## What the case proves

The first research pass looked complete but relied on vendors ranking themselves. The attempted correction replaced one interested source with another. An adversarial re-derivation refuted three claims and downgraded another when the source could not be read directly. The conclusions survived; the original evidence labels did not.

## What changes in practice

- Inspect the page behind a search result before calling a claim verified.
- Check who owns the domain and what they stand to gain.
- Show why rejected options were ruled out.
- Keep a recommendation only if it survives removal or downgrade of weak evidence.
- Harvest a real client's visual identity before designing a client-facing deliverable.

## Boundary

This is an applied research audit and teaching case. It is not merchant outcome evidence, a general platform ranking, or proof that the recommendation works for a different business.

## Next proof

Use the same audit on the next real advisory brief and record whether any recommendation changes after the source-level review.
